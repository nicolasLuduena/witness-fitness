02 · What it is

A fitness competition where the numbers are provably real.

  • Connect Strava. Stake on a fitness challenge. The contract settles it.
  • No referee, no house, no one reading your data.
↓ 2a · What “provably real” means

Not typed in. Not a screenshot. Not “trust me.”

  1. Provider returns the workout over a real TLS session.
  2. An attestor witnesses that session and produces a proof.
  3. Notaries re-check it and sign — the contract needs 2 of 3.
  4. Midnight verifies those signatures on-chain. Any handwritten number is rejected by contract.

Provably = cryptographically tied to a real API response.

07 · What Midnight actually does for us
  1. Provenance, verified on-chain. The circuit checks that 2 of 3 registered notaries signed an assertion derived from a witnessed Strava TLS session. A number a user typed in has no valid signatures and cannot enter the game.
  2. Sealing, for free. Because the check is a ZK circuit, it proves the claim is genuine without publishing the value.
  3. Real stakes, on both ledgers. The pot is unshielded NIGHT the contract escrows and pays out. The trophy is a shielded NFT only the winner can spend.
↓ 7a · Trust model, stated plainly
  • Compact cannot verify Reclaim’s circom SNARKs.
  • So the contract trusts 3 registered keys and requires ≥2 signatures.
  • TLSNotary’s own docs prescribe exactly this: “A data Verifier can also require signed data from multiple Notaries to rule out collusion between the Prover and a Notary.”
  • Today all three run on one laptop. Three organisations is the Q1 milestone.

12 · Who plays, who pays

Players

Athletes who already log everything. Run clubs, virtual races, challenge groups.

Peer-funded wagers — no house cut.

Payers

Race organisers and challenge platforms who verify manually today.

Then employer wellness, where the buyer needs verified participation and would rather not hold the data.

Corporate wellness: $55.1B, 3.1% CAGR (Grand View, 2025 — conservative of three estimates spanning $55–75B). Platform pricing anchor $0.50–$4.50 PEPM (WellSteps, published). Wellness programs cover 50M+ US workers (QJE 2019).

↓ 12a · The counter-evidence, volunteered
  • Illinois Workplace Wellness Study (QJE 2019, RCT, ~5,000 employees): null on 40 of 42 outcomes, persisting to 30 months. CIs rule out 84% of previous ROI estimates.
  • Song & Baicker (JAMA 2019, 32,974 employees): self-reported exercise +8.3 points, nothing on 10 clinical markers or 38 spending measures.
  • Both measured self-reported, unverified participation.
↓ 12b · Sizing, honestly
  • HIPAA/ACA permits wellness incentives up to 30% of coverage cost.
  • Average employer coverage $17,496/employee (Mercer, 2025).
  • → a few thousand dollars per employee per year of regulated incentive headroom, against platform pricing of $0.50–$4.50 PEPM.

The incentive pool is one to two orders of magnitude larger than the verification budget.
