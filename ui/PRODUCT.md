# Product

## Register

product

## Users

WitnessFitness primarily serves athletes who want to compete, build streaks, and prove fitness achievements without publishing their workout data or linking those achievements to a wallet identity. The hackathon experience must also work for judges seeing the product for the first time: the interface should make the contract's technical credibility, privacy guarantees, and market viability legible during a short live demonstration. Employers and wellness-program operators are secondary users who verify badge claims without receiving underlying health data.

## Product Purpose

WitnessFitness turns real, API-attested workouts into private on-chain credentials. Athletes use those credentials to enter sealed wagers, maintain streaks, mint badges, and prove achievements to third parties. Success means a first-time observer can follow the complete story—from a real Strava workout to a useful private proof—while the product remains credible as an everyday athlete experience rather than a hackathon control panel.

## Brand Personality

Calm, credible, athletic. The voice should be direct and confident, explain privacy in plain language, and let successful proofs and competition outcomes provide the energy. Technical detail should be available when it earns trust, without dominating the primary workflow.

## Anti-references

The current dense, dark, everything-at-once crypto dashboard is the explicit anti-reference. Avoid exposing all product features as equal-weight tabs, persistent infrastructure telemetry, terminal-like presentation, ornamental "sealed envelope" metaphors on every surface, and contract function names as primary action labels. The interface must not feel like a developer console, generic Web3 dashboard, fitness social feed, or employer surveillance product.

## Design Principles

1. Guide the story, preserve the product. Organize the experience around the athlete's next meaningful action while keeping every existing contract-backed capability accessible.
2. Prove value before explaining machinery. Lead with what the athlete can accomplish, then reveal commitments, notaries, transactions, and trust details progressively.
3. Make privacy concrete. At every disclosure boundary, distinguish what the athlete sees, what the chain verifies, and what a third party learns.
4. Earn technical credibility. Use real status, receipts, and failure states to support the demo narrative without letting implementation detail become the navigation model.
5. Use space to establish priority. Each page should have one primary job and enough visual separation for judges and athletes to understand state at a glance.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Maintain keyboard access, visible focus states, semantic structure, readable contrast, non-color status cues, and useful labels for technical identifiers. Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid making animation necessary to understand attestation, settlement, streak, or proof states. Use plain-language explanations so users do not need prior Midnight, zero-knowledge, or wallet expertise.
